# Non-Technical Analysis

We analyzed 29,710 emails from the Enron dataset to explore whether certain language features are used differently in phishing versus non-phishing emails.

Our research asked:  
**"Do linguistic features differ between phishing and non-phishing emails, and are those differences meaningful?"**

**Hypothesis:**  
Phishing emails use language that is statistically different from safe emails. We expected to see noticeable differences in tone, structure, or word usage.

To explore this, we compared specific features — like emotional tone, punctuation, and word choices — across both types of emails.

Among all the features tested, only one showed a strong and meaningful difference:

- **Negative emotional tone** appeared more frequently in phishing emails. This finding is statistically strong and unlikely to be due to chance.

Other differences were observed, but we cannot say with confidence that they were meaningful.

This helps us understand how phishers may rely on negative tone to influence or pressure readers.
