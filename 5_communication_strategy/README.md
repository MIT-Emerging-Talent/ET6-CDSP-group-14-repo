# Communication Strategy: Phishing Awareness Campaign

## 1. Target Audience

To ensure wide-reaching impact, we define multiple audience segments based on role, technical familiarity, and communication needs:

**Persona A: Frontline Staff** - Role: Entry- to mid-level employees across HR, Finance, Sales, Operations, and Customer Service  

- Age: All working ages  
- Tech Savviness: Basic office and email skills; limited cybersecurity awareness  
- Constraints: High email volume; short attention spans; need straightforward, visual guidance  

**Persona B: Remote & Hybrid Workers** - Role: Employees working off-site or in distributed teams  

- Tech Savviness: Varied; rely heavily on digital channels for collaboration  
- Constraints: Less in-person training; channel fatigue across tools (email, chat)  

**Persona C: Managers & Department Leads** - Role: Team leads, project managers, directors  

- Tech Savviness: Comfortable with digital tools; interested in metrics and outcomes  
- Constraints: Limited time; need high-level summaries and performance dashboards  

**Persona D: IT & Security Staff** - Role: Helpdesk, security operations, compliance teams  

- Tech Savviness: High; require technical accuracy  
- Constraints: Overloaded support queues; require concise, actionable reporting procedures

## 2. Capabilities & Constraints

| Capability                    | Constraint                                      |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------------------|
| Regularly check email inbox    | Limited time for lengthy content                |
| Access to intranet and chat    | Notification fatigue from too many alerts       |
| Familiar with short training   | Resistance to overly technical jargon           |

---

## 3. Communication Objectives

- **Awareness:** Help employees recognize common phishing tactics using clear examples  
- **Understanding:** Teach how psychological cues (urgency, fear, reward) and structural markers manifest in phishing  
- **Actionable Advice:** Provide concrete steps—hover to inspect links, verify sender domains, use internal reporting tools  
- **Reporting:** Prompt timely reporting of suspicious messages via designated channels  

---

## 4. Conveying Uncertainty & Limitations

- **Acknowledge Scope:** Clearly state that analysis is based on historical Enron data, which may not capture all current phishing variants  
- **Statistical Confidence:** Share that feature effect sizes (Cohen’s d) range from small (0.2) to large (0.5), indicating varying degrees of reliability  
- **Model Boundaries:** Emphasize ~83.5% detection accuracy—some phishing may be missed, and occasional false positives may occur  
- **Action Under Uncertainty:** Encourage employees to treat suspicious cases as potential threats even when uncertain and report rather than ignore  

---

## 5. Key Learning Takeaways

- **Red Flags in Language:** Exclamations, urgent phrases (“Act now!”), and emotional words often signal phishing  
- **Structural Cues:** Mismatched or hidden URLs, unexpected attachments, and unfamiliar sender domains  
- **Verification Steps:** Hover over links, confirm with sender via alternative channel, and consult IT support before acting  
- **Confidence Ranges:** Some tactics (e.g., URL count) are stronger indicators than others (e.g., reward words)—use combined cues for highest reliability  

---

## 6. Message & Delivery Channels

We will tailor delivery based on each persona’s preferred medium:

- **Frontline Staff (Persona A):** Weekly email bulletins and visible office posters for quick, visual updates during their regular workflows.  
- **Remote & Hybrid Workers (Persona B):** Biweekly intranet pop-ups and monthly chatbot quizzes to reach employees dispersed across locations and reduce reliance on physical materials.  
- **Managers & Department Leads (Persona C):** Email bulletins supplemented with dashboard summaries on the intranet to provide metrics and high-level insights alongside periodic lunch ‘n’ learn sessions.  
- **IT & Security Staff (Persona D):** Short, technical briefs via the chatbot and targeted workshop invites; live training ensures procedural alignment and efficient incident handling.  

| Channel         | Format                           | Frequency     | Rationale                                      |
|-----------------|----------------------------------|--------------|------------------------------------------------|
| Email Bulletin  | 1-page infographic + examples    | Weekly (Mon) | Scannable visuals fit busy schedules           |
| Intranet Pop-up | 30-second animated explainer     | Biweekly (Thu) | Engages visual learners; concise               |
| Chatbot Quiz    | Interactive scenario questions   | Monthly      | Active recall strengthens retention            |
| Lunch ‘n’ Learn | 15-minute live demo + Q&A        | Monthly (Wed) | Allows hands-on practice and discussion        |
| Office Posters  | One-sheet flyers in common areas | Quarterly    | Passive reinforcement during daily routines    |

---

## 7. Desired Audience Actions

- **Pause & Inspect:** Slow down when tone or content feels urgent or unexpected  
- **Verify:** Hover links, check sender domains, and cross-validate via a secondary channel  
- **Report:** Use <phishing@company.com> or “Report Phish” button immediately upon suspicion  
- **Engage:** Complete quick quizzes and attend workshops to reinforce best practices  

---

## 8. Success Metrics

- **Click-Through Reduction:** 50% decrease in click-throughs on simulated phishing over 3 months  
- **Reporting Rate:** 30% increase in flagged emails submitted to IT  
- **Open Rate:** ≥80% for email bulletins  
- **Quiz Completion:** ≥60% of employees complete monthly chatbot scenarios
